Product Description

Our product is a web application for renting commercial spaces, designed to streamline the rental process for small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs) and landlords. This all-in-one platform addresses common challenges, such as the lack of access to integrated tools, tedious manual processes, and inadequate cost forecasting solutions. With features like advanced property search filters, booking and leasing integration, cost estimation tools, and interactive neighborhood data, the platform empowers business owners to find the ideal rental space efficiently. For landlords, it provides property management tools to list, promote, and manage properties seamlessly. By offering a centralized, data-driven, and user-friendly solution, the platform saves time, enhances decision-making, and fosters better connections between tenants and property owners.

The idea emerged from observing the inefficiencies in the current process of renting commercial spaces. For example, café owners or retail businesses face challenges navigating multiple platforms or relying on agents, which is both time-consuming and resource-intensive. Additionally, many landlords lack modern tools for listing and managing their properties effectively.

The solution aims to bridge this gap by offering an all-in-one platform that streamlines browsing, booking, leasing, and payment processes. This unique value proposition saves time, provides data-driven insights, and delivers a seamless experience for both tenants and property owners.

Lean Canvas

PROBLEM

Small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs) face significant challenges when searching for commercial rental spaces, including:

  • Lack of a centralized platform: Businesses struggle to find and compare available properties efficiently.
  • Time-consuming manual processes: Managing inquiries, booking property visits, and handling lease agreements are tedious and prone to errors.
  • Inadequate tools for financial planning: Businesses lack tools for forecasting costs, profitability, and assessing location suitability.

Landlords and property managers encounter challenges such as:

  • Low visibility: Difficulty attracting tenants quickly due to limited exposure.
  • Inefficient property management: Managing multiple listings and tenant inquiries is often cumbersome.

CUSTOMER SEGMENT

Our target customers include:

  • SMEs and entrepreneurs: Business owners in retail, food services, office spaces, and logistics seeking accessible and affordable commercial spaces.
  • Landlords and property managers: Individuals or companies managing commercial properties who need efficient listing and management tools.

SOLUTION

Our web application offers a centralized, integrated platform for:

  1. Browsing commercial spaces: Filter properties by price, location, size, and type.
  2. Property management tools: Enable landlords to list properties, upload images, and manage pricing.
  3. Booking and leasing integration: Streamline scheduling visits and signing agreements.
  4. Cost estimation tools: Predict expenses and analyze profitability by location and business type.
  5. Interactive maps: Provide neighborhood data, foot traffic, and trends to aid decision-making.
  6. Social media integration: Promote properties via social media channels for broader reach.

An all-in-one platform that simplifies the rental process for SMEs and landlords. The platform offers exclusive property listings, advanced data insights, and seamless lease management, saving time and ensuring optimal rental decisions for business growth.

  1. Entity Relationship Diagram

        Figure 1.2 Commercia Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD)

Figure 1.2 demonstrates the Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD) which is the structure and connections between key entities in Commercia. It organizes the system into six main entities: Account, Tenant, Landlord, property, Booking, and Lease, each with its attributes and relationships.

Entities

  1. Account
  • Stores the login credentials of users, including their email and password.
  • Each account is linked to either a tenant or a landlord.
  1. Tenant
  • Contains details about tenants, such as their name and contact information.
  • A tenant can:
  • Belong to an account.
  • Make property bookings.
  • Be associated with a lease agreement for a specific property.
  1. Landlord
  • It represents landlords with attributes like name and contact information.
  • Each landlord:
  • Belongs to an account.
  • Owns one or more properties.
  1. Property
  • Represents the rental properties available in the system.
  • Attributes include property address, price, type, and availability status.
  • Each property is owned by a landlord and can be
  • Booked by tenants.
  • Leased through a formal agreement.
  1. Booking
  • Captures property booking activities made by tenants.
  • Includes details such as booking ID, date, tenant ID, and property ID.
  • Acts as a preliminary step before a lease is finalized.
  1. Lease
  • Represents the formal rental agreement between a tenant and a landlord for a specific property.
  • Attributes include the start and end dates, monthly rent, tenant ID, and property ID.
  • A lease solidifies the relationship between the tenant, landlord, and property.

Relationships Between Entities

  • Tenant and Account: A tenant belongs to an account, enabling them to log in and use the system.
  • Landlord and Account: A landlord also belongs to an account for system access.
  • Landlord and Property: A landlord can own multiple properties.
  • Tenant and Booking: A tenant can make bookings for properties.
  • Property and Booking: Each booking is associated with a specific property.
  • Tenant and Lease: A tenant enters a lease agreement for a property they book.
  • Property and Lease: A lease is directly tied to a property.

The ERD provides an overview of how tenants, landlords, and properties are connected within the system. It ensures that all interactions, such as bookings and lease agreements, are properly documented and linked to the relevant entities. This design supports efficient management of the rental process, making it easier to handle bookings, leases, and property details systematically.

This screen is divided into Tenant and Landlord sections, along with user statuses and actions. The process involves:

  1. Available Actions:
  • View: Displays detailed information about the selected user.
  • Edit: Allows admins to modify user details such as name, status, or permissions.
  • Delete: Removes the user from the system.
  1. Status Management:
  • The user’s status (e.g., "Healthy" or "Violation") indicates compliance or potential issues.
  • Admins can review and resolve violations.
  1. Other Tables:
  • Complex Table: Summarizes approvals, errors, and other system states.
